## Important Figures

### Figure 53.1: The Firm's Profit-Maximizing Quantity of Output (p. 578)
- **What it shows:** For a price-taker, profit is maximized at the output where MC crosses the horizontal MR = P line.
- **Axes / variables:** x = quantity (bushels), y = price/cost per bushel.
- **Key shapes / shifts:** MC has "swoosh" shape (falls then rises); MR is horizontal at market price.
- **Must-memorize:** Optimal point E is where MC = MR = P; produce the largest Q where MR ≥ MC if no exact crossing.

### Figure 54.1: Production Function & Total Product Curve (p. 582)
- **What it shows:** TP rises with labor at a decreasing rate (diminishing returns).
- **Axes / variables:** x = quantity of labor (workers), y = quantity of output (bushels of wheat).
- **Key shapes / shifts:** Slope of TP = MPL; flattens as MPL declines. Increasing the fixed input shifts TP up.
- **Must-memorize:** Slope of TP curve at any point = marginal product of labor (MPL).

### Figure 54.2: MPL Curve (p. 584)
- **What it shows:** MPL declines as more workers are added.
- **Axes / variables:** x = workers, y = MPL (bushels per worker).
- **Key shapes / shifts:** Downward-sloping when diminishing returns are present; shifts up if fixed input increases.
- **Must-memorize:** Diminishing returns ≠ negative returns; MPL may still be positive, just smaller.

### Figure 55.3 & 55.4: U-shaped ATC and the Cost-Curve Family (pp. 593–594)
- **What it shows:** MC crosses ATC and AVC at their respective minimums from below.
- **Axes / variables:** x = quantity, y = cost per unit.
- **Key shapes / shifts:** ATC is U-shaped (spreading effect vs. diminishing returns effect); AFC continuously falls.
- **Must-memorize:** MC = ATC at minimum ATC (analogous to grade-point average rule).

### Figure 55.6: "Swoosh" MC with U-shaped AVC and ATC (p. 596)
- **What it shows:** Realistic MC first falls (specialization) then rises (diminishing returns).
- **Axes / variables:** x = quantity, y = cost.
- **Key shapes / shifts:** MC dip → AVC U-shape; MC still passes through min ATC and min AVC.
- **Must-memorize:** "Swoosh" shape comes from initial increasing returns due to specialization.

### Figure 56.2: Short-Run vs. Long-Run ATC (p. 602)
- **What it shows:** LRATC is the lower envelope of an infinite family of SRATC curves.
- **Axes / variables:** x = quantity, y = cost per unit.
- **Key shapes / shifts:** Downward slope → economies of scale; upward slope → diseconomies of scale.
- **Must-memorize:** Each SRATC touches LRATC at one point — the output level for which that fixed cost is optimal.

### Figure 59.2: Short-Run Individual Supply Curve (p. 633)
- **What it shows:** A perfectly competitive firm's supply curve = MC above min AVC; below that, supply drops to zero.
- **Axes / variables:** x = quantity, y = price/cost.
- **Key shapes / shifts:** Break-even price = min ATC; shut-down price = min AVC.
- **Must-memorize:** Below min AVC the firm shuts down; between min AVC and min ATC it operates at a loss to cover some FC.

### Figure 60.3 / 60.4 / 60.5: Long-Run Industry Supply (pp. 643–646)
- **What it shows:** LRS is horizontal (constant cost), upward-sloping (increasing cost), or downward-sloping (decreasing cost).
- **Axes / variables:** x = industry quantity, y = price.
- **Key shapes / shifts:** Entry shifts SR supply right; LRS is always flatter (more elastic) than SR supply.
- **Must-memorize:** In long-run PC equilibrium, each firm produces at min ATC and earns zero economic profit.

### Figure 61.3: Monopoly Profit Maximization (p. 654)
- **What it shows:** Monopolist sets Q where MR = MC, then charges the price on the demand curve at that Q.
- **Axes / variables:** x = quantity, y = price/cost/MR.
- **Key shapes / shifts:** MR lies below D because of the price effect on prior units.
- **Must-memorize:** P > MR = MC for a monopolist (vs. P = MR = MC for a price-taker).

### Figure 62.1: Welfare Loss Under Monopoly (p. 660)
- **What it shows:** Monopoly transfers part of CS to producer surplus and creates a deadweight-loss triangle.
- **Axes / variables:** x = quantity, y = price.
- **Key shapes / shifts:** DWL is the yellow triangle between QM and QC, bounded by D and MC.
- **Must-memorize:** DWL exists because units where willingness-to-pay > MC go unproduced.

### Figure 62.2: Regulating a Natural Monopoly (p. 626 / printed p. 662)
- **What it shows:** Price ceiling at P*R (where D crosses ATC) eliminates monopoly profit and maximizes CS without bankrupting the firm.
- **Axes / variables:** x = quantity, y = price.
- **Key shapes / shifts:** ATC slopes downward over relevant range; setting P = MC would force losses.
- **Must-memorize:** Best regulated price for natural monopoly = where D intersects ATC (zero profit, max CS).

## Key Concepts (with mnemonics)

### Concept: Accounting Profit vs. Economic Profit
- **Definition:** Accounting profit = TR − explicit costs (and depreciation). Economic profit = TR − all opportunity costs (explicit + implicit).
- **Why it matters:** Students forget the implicit cost of the owner's time and capital. A firm can have positive accounting profit but negative economic profit.
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "Accountants Always Avoid implicit; Economists Examine Everything."
- **Related:** normal profit, implicit cost of capital, opportunity cost.

### Concept: Normal Profit
- **Definition:** Economic profit = 0; firm earns just enough to cover all opportunity costs.
- **Why it matters:** It is NOT zero accounting profit — students confuse "normal" with "nothing."
- **Mnemonic:** "Normal = Neutral; the firm is exactly indifferent between staying and leaving."
- **Related:** long-run PC equilibrium, break-even price.

### Concept: Diminishing Returns to an Input
- **Definition:** Holding all other inputs fixed, each additional unit of the variable input adds less to output.
- **Why it matters:** Confused with diseconomies of scale (which is a long-run concept where ALL inputs vary).
- **Mnemonic:** "DR is Short-run, RS is Long-run." DR = Diminishing Returns (one input fixed); RS = Returns to Scale (all inputs scale).
- **Related:** MPL curve, marginal cost rising, Malthus.

### Concept: Spreading Effect vs. Diminishing Returns Effect
- **Definition:** Spreading = AFC falling because FC is divided over more units; Diminishing returns = AVC rising because each extra unit needs more variable input.
- **Why it matters:** Together they produce U-shaped ATC: spreading dominates at low Q, diminishing returns dominates at high Q.
- **Mnemonic:** "SD curve: Spread Down at first, then Diminish Up."
- **Related:** U-shaped ATC, minimum-cost output.

### Concept: Economies of Scale vs. Diminishing Returns
- **Definition:** Economies of scale = LRATC falls as Q rises (all inputs scale). Diminishing returns = MP of one variable input falls in short run.
- **Why it matters:** Asked on AP routinely — diseconomies of scale is NOT the same as diminishing returns.
- **Mnemonic:** "Scale = Sizing the whole plant; Returns = Reaching for one more worker."
- **Related:** natural monopoly, increasing/decreasing returns to scale.

### Concept: Sunk Cost
- **Definition:** A cost already paid and unrecoverable; irrelevant to forward-looking decisions.
- **Why it matters:** Psychologically hard to ignore (loss aversion). Trick: future fixed cost is NOT sunk, but past spending is.
- **Mnemonic:** "Spilled milk — sob, don't subtract." Ignore.
- **Related:** short-run shut-down decision (FC behaves like a sunk cost in short run).

### Concept: Price-Taker vs. Price-Maker
- **Definition:** A price-taker faces a perfectly elastic (horizontal) demand at the market price; a price-maker faces the downward-sloping market demand.
- **Why it matters:** Determines whether MR = P (PC) or MR < P (monopoly, oligopoly, monopolistic comp.).
- **Mnemonic:** "Takers Take prices, Makers Move them."
- **Related:** perfect competition, demand curve shape.

### Concept: Break-Even Price vs. Shut-Down Price
- **Definition:** Break-even price = minimum ATC (zero economic profit). Shut-down price = minimum AVC (firm produces only if P ≥ this).
- **Why it matters:** Decision rule in short run differs from long run. AP loves the price-range trick.
- **Mnemonic:** "AVC = AVoid Closure; ATC = At Total Coverage." Below AVC → close.
- **Related:** short-run individual supply curve.

### Concept: Quantity Effect & Price Effect (Monopoly MR)
- **Definition:** Quantity effect = +P from selling one more unit. Price effect = −ΔP × (prior units) because monopolist must lower price on all units.
- **Why it matters:** Explains why MR < P for any price-maker (the marginal revenue curve lies BELOW the demand curve).
- **Mnemonic:** "Sell one More, Mark down all the Rest." More + Markdown = MR.
- **Related:** monopoly graph, oligopoly, monopolistic competition.

### Concept: Allocative vs. Productive Efficiency
- **Definition:** Allocative efficiency = P = MC (producing the socially optimal mix). Productive efficiency = producing at minimum ATC (no resource waste).
- **Why it matters:** PC achieves BOTH in long run; monopoly achieves NEITHER (P > MC, and Q is below min-ATC quantity if MR-MC crossing is at a smaller Q).
- **Mnemonic:** "Allocate at the Margin; Produce at the Minimum."
- **Related:** deadweight loss, long-run PC equilibrium.

### Concept: Natural Monopoly
- **Definition:** A single firm has lower ATC than multiple firms over the relevant output range, due to large fixed costs and economies of scale.
- **Why it matters:** Anti-trust action could RAISE costs; instead regulate to P = ATC.
- **Mnemonic:** "Naturally Needs Network — big pipes, one provider."
- **Related:** local utilities, average-cost pricing regulation.

### Concept: Herfindahl–Hirschman Index (HHI)
- **Definition:** Sum of squared market shares (in percent) of all firms in the industry. Range: ~0 (PC) to 10,000 (pure monopoly).
- **Why it matters:** Squaring weights larger firms more heavily; AP may ask you to compute or interpret.
- **Mnemonic:** "HHI = Heavy on the Hefty Incumbents."
- **Related:** concentration ratio, oligopoly identification.

## Important Formulas

### Formula: Economic Profit
- **Equation:** Economic Profit = TR − (Explicit Cost + Implicit Cost)
- **Variables:** TR = total revenue; explicit cost = cash outlays; implicit cost = opportunity cost of owned resources.
- **Units / sign conventions:** Can be negative (loss). Zero economic profit = normal profit.
- **Worked example:** TR = $100,000; Explicit = $65,000 (incl. depreciation); Implicit = $37,000 → Economic profit = −$2,000 (firm should consider exit).

### Formula: Marginal Revenue
- **Equation:** MR = ΔTR / ΔQ
- **Variables:** ΔTR = change in total revenue; ΔQ = change in quantity.
- **Units / sign conventions:** Dollars per unit. For PC firms MR = P (constant). For monopoly MR < P and can be negative.
- **Worked example:** Monopolist sells 9 diamonds at $550 (TR=$4,950); 10 at $500 (TR=$5,000). MR of 10th = $50.

### Formula: Marginal Cost
- **Equation:** MC = ΔTC / ΔQ
- **Variables:** ΔTC = change in total cost; ΔQ = change in quantity.
- **Units / sign conventions:** Dollars per unit. Equal to slope of TC curve.
- **Worked example:** TC at Q = 4 is $300; at Q = 5 is $408. MC of 5th unit = $108.

### Formula: Optimal Output Rule
- **Equation:** Produce until MR = MC. For PC: P = MC. For monopoly: MR = MC (and then read price off demand curve).
- **Variables:** MR = marginal revenue; MC = marginal cost; P = market price.
- **Units / sign conventions:** If no exact crossing, produce largest Q where MR ≥ MC.
- **Worked example:** PC firm with P = $18: MC values are $16,$6,$8,$12,$16,$20… Produce 5 (MC = $16 < P; 6th MC = $20 > P).

### Formula: Total Cost / Average Total Cost
- **Equation:** TC = FC + VC; ATC = TC/Q = AFC + AVC = (FC/Q) + (VC/Q)
- **Variables:** FC fixed, VC variable, Q output.
- **Units / sign conventions:** AFC always falls with Q; AVC eventually rises.
- **Worked example:** FC = $108, Q = 4, VC = $192 → TC = $300, ATC = $75, AFC = $27, AVC = $48.

### Formula: Profit Rectangle
- **Equation:** Profit = (P − ATC) × Q
- **Variables:** P = price (read off demand at Q*); ATC = average total cost at Q*; Q = profit-maximizing output.
- **Units / sign conventions:** Negative if P < ATC (loss). For monopoly use P_M and ATC at Q_M.
- **Worked example:** Monopolist: P_M=$600, ATC=$200, Q_M=8 → Profit = (600−200)·8 = $3,200.

### Formula: Marginal Product of Labor
- **Equation:** MPL = ΔQ / ΔL
- **Variables:** ΔQ = change in output; ΔL = change in labor.
- **Units / sign conventions:** Output units per worker. Slope of TP curve.
- **Worked example:** Output rises from 64 to 75 bushels when L goes from 4 to 5 workers; MPL of 5th worker = 11 bushels.

### Formula: Herfindahl–Hirschman Index
- **Equation:** HHI = Σ (sᵢ)² where sᵢ is firm i's market-share percentage.
- **Variables:** sᵢ in percent (NOT decimals).
- **Units / sign conventions:** Max 10,000 (one firm with 100% share squared). Above ~2,500 → highly concentrated.
- **Worked example:** Three firms with 60%, 25%, 15% shares → HHI = 60² + 25² + 15² = 3,600 + 625 + 225 = 4,450.

### Formula: Concentration Ratio
- **Equation:** CRₓ = sum of market shares of the X largest firms.
- **Variables:** X usually 4 or 8.
- **Units / sign conventions:** Percent (0–100).
- **Worked example:** Top 4 firms have 25%, 20%, 15%, 10% → CR4 = 70.

## Practice Questions

### Q1 [EASY] [CONCEPTUAL] — Economic vs. Accounting Profit
**Question:** A bakery has total revenue of $80,000, explicit costs of $50,000 (including depreciation), and the owner could earn $25,000 elsewhere and $4,000 in interest on the equipment if sold. What is the economic profit, and what should the owner do in the long run?
**Choices:** A) +$30,000; stay open. B) +$5,000; stay open. C) +$1,000; stay open. D) −$1,000; close. E) Cannot determine.
**Correct:** C
**Explanation:** Economic profit = 80,000 − 50,000 − 25,000 − 4,000 = +$1,000. Slightly positive, so the owner should stay open. Common error: forgetting either the implicit cost of the owner's time OR the implicit cost of capital. Answer A confuses accounting profit with economic profit.

### Q2 [EASY] [NUMERICAL] — Marginal Cost
**Question:** A firm produces output where TC at Q=3 is $216 and TC at Q=4 is $300. The marginal cost of the 4th unit is:
**Choices:** A) $54. B) $75. C) $84. D) $108. E) $216.
**Correct:** C
**Explanation:** MC = ΔTC/ΔQ = (300 − 216)/1 = $84. Trick: A is ATC at Q = 4; B is ATC; D is AFC + something; pure distractors.

### Q3 [MEDIUM] [CONCEPTUAL] — Shut-Down vs. Break-Even
**Question:** A perfectly competitive firm sees the market price drop to $12. Its minimum ATC is $14 and its minimum AVC is $10. In the short run, the firm should:
**Choices:** A) Shut down immediately because it's losing money. B) Produce where P = MC, even though it loses money, because it covers all variable cost and some fixed cost. C) Shut down because P < ATC. D) Raise its price to $14. E) Produce at minimum ATC.
**Correct:** B
**Explanation:** P ($12) > min AVC ($10), so operating loses less than shutting down. P < min ATC, so there IS a loss, but partial fixed-cost coverage beats paying full FC while idle. Common trap A/C: students automatically shut down whenever P < ATC.

### Q4 [MEDIUM] [NUMERICAL] — Monopoly Profit
**Question:** A monopolist with constant MC = ATC = $20 faces a demand curve where Q* (from MR = MC) is 50 and the corresponding price on the demand curve is $60. What is the monopolist's profit, and what is the deadweight loss if the perfectly competitive output would be 100?
**Choices:** A) Profit = $2,000; DWL = $1,000. B) Profit = $3,000; DWL = $1,000. C) Profit = $2,000; DWL = $2,000. D) Profit = $1,000; DWL = $500. E) Profit = $3,000; DWL = $2,000.
**Correct:** A
**Explanation:** Profit = (P − ATC) × Q = (60 − 20) × 50 = $2,000. DWL is a triangle with base (100 − 50) = 50 and height (60 − 20) = $40; area = ½ × 50 × 40 = $1,000. Trick: students forget the ½ factor in the triangle area (gives B).

### Q5 [MEDIUM] [CONCEPTUAL] — Returns to Scale
**Question:** A factory doubles ALL its inputs and finds output rises by 150%. This factory exhibits:
**Choices:** A) Diminishing returns to labor. B) Increasing returns to scale. C) Constant returns to scale. D) Decreasing returns to scale. E) Economies of scope.
**Correct:** B
**Explanation:** Output more than doubled (150% > 100%), so increasing returns to scale → economies of scale. Trick A: students confuse diminishing returns (short-run, one input fixed) with returns to scale (long-run, all inputs vary).

### Q6 [MEDIUM] [NUMERICAL] — HHI Calculation
**Question:** An industry has 4 firms with market shares of 40%, 30%, 20%, and 10%. Compute the HHI and the four-firm concentration ratio.
**Correct:** HHI = 1,600 + 900 + 400 + 100 = 3,000; CR4 = 100.
**Explanation:** Square each share, then sum (units = percent²). CR4 sums the unsquared shares of the top 4 firms. Common error: using decimals (0.4² = 0.16) gives 0.30 instead of 3,000; that wrong scale confuses students about the 0–10,000 HHI range.

### Q7 [HARD] [CONCEPTUAL] — MC vs. ATC Geometry
**Question:** A firm's marginal cost curve crosses its average total cost curve at $25. At an output of 100 units, marginal cost is $30 and average variable cost is $22. Which of the following must be true?
**Choices:** A) ATC at Q=100 is less than $25 because MC has risen. B) ATC at Q=100 is greater than $25 because MC > ATC at the minimum and MC continues to pull ATC up. C) AFC at Q=100 equals $8. D) The firm is at minimum AVC. E) The firm should shut down.
**Correct:** B
**Explanation:** Once MC ($30) is above the previous minimum ATC ($25), each additional unit pulls ATC up, so ATC must be rising above $25 at Q = 100. C is a trap: we cannot pin AFC because we don't know ATC. D fails because at min AVC, MC = AVC; here MC ($30) ≠ AVC ($22). E ignores price.

### Q8 [HARD] [NUMERICAL] — Long-Run PC Equilibrium
**Question:** In a constant-cost perfectly competitive industry, each firm's long-run ATC reaches a minimum of $14 at Q = 4 bushels. Market demand at $14 is 1,000 bushels. In long-run equilibrium, how many firms are in the industry and what economic profit does each earn?
**Correct:** 250 firms; zero economic profit each.
**Explanation:** Long-run PC equilibrium → each firm produces at min ATC (Q = 4) and earns zero economic profit. Industry quantity = 1,000, so firms = 1,000 / 4 = 250. Trick: confusion between "normal profit" (zero economic) and "no profit at all" — accounting profit may be positive even though economic profit is zero.

### Q9 [HARD] [CONCEPTUAL] — Sunk Cost Trap
**Question:** A movie theater paid $500,000 last year for a non-refundable, non-resaleable license to show "Megahit 7." Ticket sales bring in $20,000/week, but variable operating costs are $25,000/week. The theater is debating whether to keep showing the movie or switch to "Cute Indie" (variable cost $18,000/wk, expected revenue $19,000/wk). Which should it choose, and why?
**Choices:** A) Keep showing Megahit 7 to recoup the $500,000 license. B) Switch to Cute Indie because it brings positive contribution margin while Megahit 7 brings negative. C) Close the theater. D) Switch to Cute Indie only if it can resell the license. E) Indifferent — both are losing money.
**Correct:** B
**Explanation:** The $500,000 is SUNK and irrelevant. Compare contribution margins: Megahit −$5,000/wk (operates below shut-down price); Cute Indie +$1,000/wk. Switch. Trap A is the classic "sunk-cost fallacy."

### Q10 [OLYMPIAD] [CONCEPTUAL] — Lump-Sum vs. Per-Unit Tax on Monopoly
**Question:** A profitable monopolist faces an excise tax of $T per unit. Separately, suppose instead the government imposes a lump-sum tax of $L on the same monopolist (small enough that the firm continues operating). Which statements are true?
I. The per-unit tax raises marginal cost and reduces equilibrium Q; deadweight loss increases.
II. The lump-sum tax leaves Q and P unchanged; deadweight loss is unchanged.
III. The lump-sum tax is a form of price regulation.
IV. Both taxes reduce monopoly profit.
**Choices:** A) I only. B) I and II only. C) I, II, and IV only. D) I, II, III, and IV. E) II and IV only.
**Correct:** C
**Explanation:** A per-unit tax shifts MC up, lowers Q, raises P, adds to DWL (I correct). A lump-sum tax is independent of Q, so it doesn't change MR or MC; profit-maximizing Q stays the same and DWL is unchanged (II correct). III is false — a lump-sum tax is not price regulation; it just transfers profit. IV is correct because both reduce profit (the per-unit tax raises costs at all Q, and the lump-sum tax directly subtracts from profit). Common trap: students think the monopolist will raise the price after a lump-sum tax to "recover" the tax — it won't, because the profit-maximizing P is already chosen.

### Q11 [OLYMPIAD] [NUMERICAL] — Regulated Natural Monopoly
**Question:** A natural monopoly faces inverse demand P = 100 − Q. Its costs are TC = 400 + 20Q (so MC = 20, ATC = 400/Q + 20). Calculate (a) the unregulated monopoly Q and P, (b) the socially optimal (P = MC) Q (and the loss the firm would incur there), and (c) the "average-cost" regulated price and quantity where the firm just breaks even.
**Correct:** 
(a) MR = 100 − 2Q; set MR = MC: 100 − 2Q = 20 → Q = 40, P = 60. Profit = (60 − 30) × 40 = $1,200, where ATC at Q=40 is 400/40+20 = 30.
(b) P = MC: 20 = 100 − Q → Q = 80, P = 20. ATC at Q=80 = 400/80 + 20 = 25. Loss per unit = 5, total loss = $400 (so the firm would refuse to operate without a subsidy).
(c) Set P = ATC: 100 − Q = 400/Q + 20 → 80Q − Q² = 400 → Q² − 80Q + 400 = 0 → Q = (80 ± √(6400 − 1600))/2 = (80 ± √4800)/2 ≈ (80 ± 69.28)/2. Take Q ≈ 74.64 (the larger root, where demand meets ATC on the lower side); P ≈ 25.36; profit ≈ 0.
**Explanation:** This illustrates the trade-off: (b) is allocatively efficient but bankrupts the firm. (c) is the practical "second-best" — maximum CS subject to firm break-even. Trap: students often set P = MC and forget that natural monopolies with declining ATC cannot cover fixed costs at P = MC.

### Q12 [OLYMPIAD] [CONCEPTUAL] — Market-Structure Identification
**Question:** An industry has 6 firms with market shares 30%, 25%, 20%, 15%, 7%, 3%. Products are slightly differentiated by brand. Entry requires moderate fixed investment and government licensing. Choose the BEST market structure description AND justify with HHI.
**Correct:** Oligopoly (with elements of differentiated-product oligopoly).
HHI = 900 + 625 + 400 + 225 + 49 + 9 = 2,208. CR4 = 90.
**Explanation:** HHI above 2,500 is "highly concentrated," but 2,208 is still very high, with CR4 = 90 indicating four firms dominate. Differentiated products + few firms + barriers to entry = oligopoly with product differentiation (not monopolistic competition, because the latter requires MANY firms and easy entry). Common error: students see "differentiated products" and pick monopolistic competition, ignoring the small number of firms and entry barriers.

## High-Yield Synthesis
- **MR = MC is universal.** Every profit-maximizing firm — perfectly competitive, monopoly, oligopoly, monopolistic competitor — sets MR = MC. The DIFFERENCE across market structures is how MR relates to price: PC has MR = P (horizontal demand); all price-makers have MR < P (downward-sloping demand creates a price effect). Memorize this and you can derive every market-structure outcome.
- **The cost-curve geometry is non-negotiable AP material.** ATC is U-shaped (spreading vs. diminishing-returns effects); MC cuts ATC and AVC from below at their respective minimums; the gap between ATC and AVC is AFC, which shrinks as Q grows. In the long run, LRATC is the envelope of SRATC curves and economies/diseconomies of scale (NOT diminishing returns) drive its shape.
- **Short-run vs. long-run decisions hinge on three benchmark prices.** Shut down if P < min AVC (short run). Produce at a loss if min AVC ≤ P < min ATC (short run only). Earn positive economic profit if P > min ATC (and entry will erode it in PC long-run equilibrium). Monopolies are the exception: barriers to entry preserve profit indefinitely, creating deadweight loss that justifies antitrust or price-regulation policy.
