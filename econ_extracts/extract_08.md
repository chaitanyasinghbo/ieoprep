## Key Concepts (with mnemonics)

### Concept: Single-Price Monopolist vs. Price Discrimination
- **Definition:** A single-price monopolist charges all consumers the same price; a price-discriminating monopolist charges different prices to different consumers for the SAME good.
- **Why it matters:** Students confuse price discrimination with illegal practices; it's actually legal in most forms and INCREASES efficiency.
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "Discriminate by Demand Elasticity" — charge inelastic buyers MORE.
- **Related:** perfect price discrimination, two-part tariff, volume discount, advance-purchase restriction.

### Concept: Perfect Price Discrimination
- **Definition:** Each consumer pays exactly their willingness to pay; ALL consumer surplus becomes producer profit.
- **Why it matters:** It is THE one monopoly case with zero deadweight loss — same Q as perfect competition!
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "Perfect PD = Perfect Q (efficient), Perfect Zero CS (zero surplus to buyers)."
- **Related:** willingness to pay, allocative efficiency.

### Concept: Conditions for Price Discrimination
- **Definition:** Firm must (1) have market power, (2) identify groups with different elasticities, (3) prevent resale.
- **Mnemonic:** "PER" — **P**ower, **E**lasticity differences, **R**esale prevention.

### Concept: Oligopoly + Interdependence + Duopoly
- **Definition:** Few firms whose profits depend on each other's actions. A duopoly has two firms.
- **Mnemonic:** "Oligo = OH-NO they're watching me." Each firm watches what rivals do.
- **Related:** Herfindahl-Hirschman Index, 4-firm concentration ratio.

### Concept: Collusion vs. Cartel
- **Definition:** Collusion = firms cooperate to raise joint profit. Cartel = formal collusive agreement.
- **Why it matters:** Cartels are ILLEGAL in U.S. and EU; OPEC is legal only because it's a government cartel.
- **Mnemonic:** "Cartel = Contract"; "Collusion = Cooperation."

### Concept: Quantity Effect vs. Price Effect
- **Definition:** Selling one more unit gives +P (quantity effect) but forces a price cut on all units (negative price effect).
- **Why it matters:** The price effect is why MR < P for monopolists. Each oligopolist only feels the price effect on ITS OWN output, not its rivals' → individual incentive to cheat on collusive output.
- **Mnemonic:** "Q-up, P-down" — both effects always present for downward-sloping demand.

### Concept: Prisoners' Dilemma
- **Definition:** A game where each player's dominant strategy makes both worse off than mutual cooperation.
- **Mnemonic:** "I Don't Trust You, You Don't Trust Me, We Both Lose." (IDTYYDTMWBL)
- **Related:** Nash equilibrium, dominant strategy, repeated games.

### Concept: Dominant Strategy
- **Definition:** A player's best action regardless of what the opponent does.
- **Mnemonic:** "Dominant = always Done first." If circles cluster in one row (or column), it's the dominant strategy for that player.
- **Trick:** Not every game has a dominant strategy. Every dominant-strategy equilibrium is a Nash equilibrium, but not vice versa.

### Concept: Nash Equilibrium (Noncooperative Equilibrium)
- **Definition:** A strategy profile where no player wants to unilaterally change strategy.
- **Mnemonic:** "Nash = Nobody Adjusts, Solo-decision Holds."
- **Related:** A game may have 0, 1, or multiple Nash equilibria.

### Concept: Tit-for-Tat
- **Definition:** Cooperate first, then mimic opponent's previous move. Rewards cooperation, punishes cheating.
- **Mnemonic:** "Tit = Treat them as they Treat you."
- **Why it matters:** Supports tacit collusion in REPEATED games even though individual cheating is the one-shot Nash equilibrium.

### Concept: Tacit Collusion
- **Definition:** Firms maintain high prices without formal agreement, often via repeated interaction or price leadership.
- **Mnemonic:** "Tacit = Together but silent."

### Concept: Antitrust Policy
- **Definition:** Government efforts to prevent oligopolies from monopolizing or fixing prices. (Sherman Antitrust Act, 1890.)
- **Mnemonic:** "Anti-Trust = Anti-Cartel."
- **Trick fact:** The first federal antitrust law passed in **1890** (Sherman). Standard Oil was broken up in **1911**.

### Concept: Price War, Price Leadership, Nonprice Competition
- **Price war:** Tacit collusion breaks down → prices collapse to MC.
- **Price leadership:** One firm sets price; others follow (e.g., 1950s–60s GM in autos).
- **Nonprice competition:** Advertising, features, branding to compete without lowering prices.
- **Mnemonic:** "When you can't COMPETE on price, COMPETE on EVERYTHING ELSE."

### Concept: Monopolistic Competition
- **Definition:** Many firms, differentiated products, free entry/exit, no barriers, downward-sloping firm demand.
- **Mnemonic:** "Monop-Comp = Many Monopolists" — each is a tiny monopolist over its own niche.
- **Trick:** In LR, P = ATC > MC, AND Q < min-ATC Q. Both allocatively AND productively inefficient.

### Concept: Excess Capacity
- **Definition:** Monop. comp. firms in LR produce LESS than the quantity that minimizes ATC.
- **Mnemonic:** "Excess capacity = Empty seats" (e.g., food court vendors are never fully scaled up).

### Concept: Product Differentiation (3 Types)
- **By style/type, by location, by quality.**
- **Mnemonic:** "SLQ — Style, Location, Quality" or "SeLeQt."

### Concept: Derived Demand
- **Definition:** Demand for an input is derived from demand for the output it produces.
- **Mnemonic:** "Demand drips DOWN — output demand → input demand."

### Concept: Marginal Revenue Product (MRP)
- **Definition:** Additional revenue from one more unit of an input = MPL × MR (or P, if PC product market).
- **Why it matters:** A firm's MRP curve IS its factor demand curve.

### Concept: Marginal Factor Cost (MFC)
- **Definition:** The additional cost of one more unit of an input. In PC labor markets, MFC = wage. In monopsony, MFC > wage (because hiring one more raises wages for ALL workers).
- **Mnemonic:** "Monopsony's MFC is Mighty More than the wage."

### Concept: Economic Rent
- **Definition:** Payment to a factor in excess of the minimum needed to employ it.
- **Mnemonic:** "Rent = Reward beyond Required."
- **Related:** Producer surplus is the goods-market analog. Land tax has no DWL because supply is fixed.

### Concept: Backward-Bending Labor Supply
- **Definition:** At low wages, substitution effect dominates → more labor supplied. At high wages, income effect dominates → less labor supplied.
- **Mnemonic:** "Bend at the Bonus" — when wages get really high, you'd rather take vacation.

### Concept: Monopsony
- **Definition:** A single buyer of an input. Pays a wage BELOW competitive wage and hires LESS labor than competitive equilibrium.
- **Mnemonic:** "Mono-PSO-ny = One-Pays-Squat-Only."
- **Trick:** Wage is read off the SUPPLY curve at L*, not at the MFC = MRP intersection.

### Concept: Cost-Minimization Rule
- **Definition:** Adjust inputs until MP per dollar is equal across all factors: MPL/W = MPK/r.
- **Mnemonic:** "Bang per buck must be balanced."
- **Trick:** If MPL/W > MPK/r, hire MORE labor (cheaper bang); diminishing returns will bring ratios back to equal.

### Concept: Marginal Productivity Theory of Income Distribution
- **Definition:** In competitive factor markets, each factor is paid the equilibrium MRP of the last unit hired.
- **Why it matters:** Explains WHY labor gets ~70% — labor's equilibrium MRP × quantity = ~70% of total income.

### Concept: Compensating Differentials
- **Definition:** Wage premiums (or discounts) for unpleasant/dangerous (or pleasant) jobs of equivalent skill.
- **Mnemonic:** "Compensate for Crap" — dirtier/more-dangerous job → higher pay.

### Concept: Efficiency Wages
- **Definition:** Above-equilibrium wages paid to motivate workers and reduce turnover.
- **Why it matters:** Creates a wage floor → labor surplus → some unemployment.
- **Mnemonic:** "Efficiency wages = Extra to Encourage Effort."

### Concept: Discrimination & Market Forces
- **Key insight:** Competitive markets push AGAINST discrimination (non-discriminating firms have a cost advantage). Discrimination persists due to (1) market imperfections (wages above equilibrium, e.g., efficiency wages or union floors) and (2) institutionalization (laws like apartheid).

## Important Formulas

### Formula: Marginal Revenue Product of Labor
- **Equation:** MRPL = MPL × MR (in PC product market: MRPL = MPL × P)
- **Variables:** MPL = marginal product of labor (units/worker); MR = marginal revenue ($/unit); P = price.
- **Units / sign conventions:** Always in $/worker. Slopes downward due to diminishing returns (and, for non-PC firms, the price effect).
- **Worked example:** If the 3rd worker produces 18 pizzas and MR per pizza is $9.69, MRPL of 3rd worker = 18 × $9.69 = $169.74.

### Formula: Profit-Maximizing Factor Hiring Rule
- **Equation:** Hire factor until MRP = MFC (in PC labor: MRPL = W).
- **Variables:** MRP of last factor unit; MFC = wage in PC, > wage in monopsony.
- **Gotcha:** In monopsony, find Q* where MRP = MFC, then read W* off the SUPPLY curve (below MFC).
- **Worked example:** George/Martha pay W = $200. From their table, MRPL of 5th worker = $220, of 6th worker = $180. They hire 5 workers (the last worker with MRPL ≥ W).

### Formula: Cost-Minimization Rule
- **Equation:** MPL / W = MPK / r
- **Variables:** MPL, MPK = marginal products of labor and capital; W = wage; r = rental rate.
- **Gotcha:** If MPL/W > MPK/r, substitute labor for capital (more bang per buck on labor).
- **Worked example:** MPL=400, MPK=1,000, W=$20, r=$100. MPL/W = 20, MPK/r = 10. Since 20 > 10, hire MORE labor (and rent less capital) until they equalize.

### Formula: Cartel Profit-Maximizing Output
- **Equation:** Set MR = MC for the JOINT cartel (treat as monopoly).
- **Gotcha:** Each member firm has an incentive to cheat and produce more than its allocated share, because the negative price effect spreads over the whole cartel but the firm captures the full quantity effect.
- **Worked example:** Lysine demand: P=$6 at Q=60 → TR=$360M is max. Cartel sets Q=60M, splits 30M each, $180M revenue each. If one cheats and produces 40M while other produces 30M: total Q=70M → P=$5, cheater earns $200M (gains $20M), honest firm earns $150M.

### Formula: Equilibrium Marginal Revenue Product
- **Equation:** Each factor unit in a competitive market earns its equilibrium MRP, which is the MRP of the LAST unit hired in the entire market (not the average MRP).
- **Worked example:** If average machinist generates $137K in revenue but the last hired generates ~$70K, the equilibrium wage is closer to $70K (plus benefits/training costs).

## Practice Questions

### Q1 [EASY] [CONCEPTUAL] — Price discrimination
**Question:** Which of the following is NOT required for a firm to engage in price discrimination?
**Choices:** A) Market power B) Ability to identify groups with different elasticities C) Ability to prevent resale between groups D) A horizontal demand curve E) Differentiation of buyers by willingness to pay
**Correct:** D
**Explanation:** A horizontal demand curve means perfectly elastic demand (perfect competition), which precludes any market power. Price discrimination REQUIRES a downward-sloping demand curve. The other three (A, B, C) are the classic "PER" conditions.

### Q2 [MEDIUM] [NUMERICAL] — Perfect price discrimination
**Question:** A monopolist faces a linear demand: P = 100 − Q. MC = 20 (constant). Compare profits under (i) single-price monopoly and (ii) perfect price discrimination.
**Correct:** Single-price: MR = 100 − 2Q = 20 → Q = 40, P = 60, profit = (60−20)(40) = $1,600. Perfect PD: produce until P = MC → Q = 80; profit = area of triangle = ½(100−20)(80) = $3,200.
**Explanation:** Perfect PD doubles profit AND eliminates DWL. Note: under PD, consumer surplus = 0; under single-price, CS = ½(100−60)(40) = $800, DWL = ½(60−20)(80−40) = $800. PD recovers BOTH the CS triangle and the DWL.

### Q3 [MEDIUM] [CONCEPTUAL] — Prisoners' dilemma trick
**Question:** Two firms can each set High or Low prices. Payoffs (Firm A, Firm B): (H,H)=($3M, $3M); (H,L)=($1M, $4M); (L,H)=($4M, $1M); (L,L)=($2M, $2M). What is the Nash equilibrium, and is it Pareto-efficient?
**Choices:** A) (H,H); efficient B) (L,L); efficient C) (L,L); inefficient D) (H,L); inefficient E) Multiple Nash equilibria exist
**Correct:** C
**Explanation:** Each firm's dominant strategy is Low (4 > 3 if other plays H; 2 > 1 if other plays L). So Nash = (L,L) with payoffs ($2M, $2M). But (H,H) gives ($3M, $3M) — Pareto-superior! That's the dilemma. A trick answer would be B; (L,L) is NOT efficient because both firms would prefer (H,H).

### Q4 [MEDIUM] [CONCEPTUAL] — Monopolistic competition long-run
**Question:** In long-run equilibrium for a monopolistically competitive firm, which of the following is TRUE?
**Choices:** A) P = MC = ATC and Q is at min ATC B) P = ATC, but P > MC, and Q < min-ATC Q C) P > ATC and economic profit is positive D) P = MR = MC and ATC is irrelevant E) D is below ATC at all quantities
**Correct:** B
**Explanation:** In LR monop. comp., entry shifts D leftward until D is tangent to ATC at the profit-max Q. So P = ATC (zero profit), but tangency occurs on the downward-sloping (left) portion of ATC, so Q < min-cost Q (excess capacity), and P > MC (allocative inefficiency). Trick: A describes PC, not monop. comp.

### Q5 [HARD] [NUMERICAL] — Marginal revenue product
**Question:** A firm in a perfectly competitive product market sells at P=$15. Labor (workers): 0→0; 1→300; 2→550; 3→700; 4→800; 5→850; 6→890 units. If the wage is $1,800 per worker, how many workers should the firm hire?
**Correct:** 4 workers.
**Explanation:** Compute MPL then MRPL = MPL × $15. MPL: 300, 250, 150, 100, 50, 40. MRPL: $4,500, $3,750, $2,250, $1,500, $750, $600. Hire while MRPL ≥ W=$1,800: worker 1 (✓), 2 (✓), 3 (✓), 4 ($1,500 < $1,800 → STOP at 4? Actually 4th MRPL = $1,500 < $1,800 so NO). The 3rd worker MRPL=$2,250 > $1,800. The 4th = $1,500 < $1,800. Stop at 3. **Correction: hire 3.** (Trick: many students miscalculate MPL—watch the table carefully.)

### Q6 [HARD] [CONCEPTUAL] — Monopsony wage
**Question:** A monopsonist faces an upward-sloping labor supply curve. If the firm hires the profit-maximizing quantity of labor, the wage paid will be:
**Choices:** A) Equal to MRPL B) Equal to MFCL C) Less than MRPL D) Greater than MFCL E) Equal to the perfectly competitive equilibrium wage
**Correct:** C
**Explanation:** In monopsony, Q* is where MRPL = MFCL. But MFCL > W (since hiring one more raises pay for all), so MRPL > W. The firm pays a wage read off the SUPPLY curve at Q*, which is below MRPL. The worker is "exploited" — paid less than the marginal revenue they produce. (A) is wrong because that holds only in PC labor markets.

### Q7 [HARD] [NUMERICAL] — Cost minimization rule
**Question:** A firm produces using labor (W = $40) and capital (r = $100). At its current input mix, MPL = 200 and MPK = 400. Is the firm cost-minimizing? If not, what should it do?
**Correct:** Not cost-minimizing. MPL/W = 200/40 = 5; MPK/r = 400/100 = 4. Since MPL/W > MPK/r, the firm should HIRE MORE LABOR and RENT LESS CAPITAL.
**Explanation:** Each $ spent on labor gives 5 units of output; each $ spent on capital gives 4. Substitute labor for capital. As more labor is hired, MPL falls (diminishing returns); as less capital is rented, MPK rises. Continue until MPL/W = MPK/r.

### Q8 [OLYMPIAD] [CONCEPTUAL] — Sequential vs. simultaneous prisoners' dilemma
**Question:** In a one-shot simultaneous prisoners' dilemma, both players defect at Nash equilibrium. Now suppose the game is played sequentially: Player 1 moves first, Player 2 observes and then chooses. With standard PD payoffs, what is the subgame-perfect equilibrium, and is it different from the simultaneous game?
**Correct:** Same outcome: both defect. Subgame-perfect equilibrium: Player 1 anticipates that Player 2 will defect regardless of Player 1's choice (defection is dominant for Player 2). Given that, Player 1 also defects. Both defect.
**Explanation:** Sequential play doesn't help when defection is a DOMINANT strategy — observing opponent's move doesn't change one's best response. The dilemma resolves only with (i) finite repetition with uncertain end, (ii) infinite repetition, or (iii) binding commitment. Trick: many think "if I see them cooperate, I can cooperate too" — but that ignores that defection still pays more for player 2.

### Q9 [OLYMPIAD] [NUMERICAL] — Cartel cheating incentive (Cournot intuition)
**Question:** Two duopolists face P = 24 − Q where Q = q1 + q2, and MC = 0 for both. (a) What is the joint-profit-maximizing total output, and how much profit per firm if they split evenly? (b) Suppose firm 1 honors the agreement but firm 2 cheats and produces such that q2 maximizes its individual profit, taking q1 as fixed. How much does firm 2 produce, and what is its profit?
**Correct:** (a) Joint TR = (24−Q)Q; max at Q=12, P=$12, joint profit = $144, $72 each (q1 = q2 = 6). (b) With q1 = 6 fixed, firm 2 maximizes π2 = (24 − 6 − q2)q2 = (18 − q2)q2 → q2 = 9. Then Q = 15, P = $9, π2 = 9 × $9 = $81.
**Explanation:** Firm 2's cheating profit ($81) > collusive profit ($72). Firm 1's profit collapses to 6 × $9 = $54. Both firms see this incentive, so if both cheat: Cournot-Nash with q1 = q2 = 8 → Q = 16, P = $8, each earns $64 — worse than collusion ($72) but better than being the lone honest one ($54).

### Q10 [OLYMPIAD] [CONCEPTUAL] — Land tax & efficiency
**Question:** Economist Henry George argued for a "single tax" on land. Using marginal productivity theory, explain why a tax on the rental value of unimproved land creates no deadweight loss, while a tax on labor or capital does. Then identify ONE practical reason such a tax is rare.
**Correct:** Land in fixed supply means the supply curve is vertical. A tax shifts demand (or reduces post-tax rental rate to landowners) but does NOT change Q transacted, since landowners cannot reduce supply in response. No mutually beneficial trades are eliminated → no DWL. Labor and capital have upward-sloping supplies, so taxes reduce Q and create DWL. Practical reason: difficulty of separating "unimproved land value" from improvements (buildings, irrigation), valuation disputes, political opposition from wealthy landowners.
**Explanation:** This is the classic Georgist argument. The intuition: incidence falls 100% on the inelastic side. With perfectly inelastic supply, landowners bear the full tax and the equilibrium quantity is unchanged. Many economists (including Milton Friedman) called land taxes the "least bad" tax.

### Q11 [MEDIUM] [CONCEPTUAL] — Backward-bending labor supply trick
**Question:** A worker's wage doubles and she chooses to work FEWER hours. This implies that for her:
**Choices:** A) Leisure is an inferior good B) The substitution effect dominates the income effect C) The income effect dominates the substitution effect D) The substitution effect and income effect are equal E) She is irrational
**Correct:** C
**Explanation:** When wages rise, the substitution effect always says "work more" (leisure is now more expensive). The income effect says "work less" (she's richer; leisure is normal). If she works LESS, income effect must dominate. (A) is wrong: leisure is normal — that's what creates the negative income effect on labor supply.

### Q12 [HARD] [CONCEPTUAL] — Efficiency wages
**Question:** Why might a firm voluntarily pay a wage ABOVE the market-clearing wage, and what is the macroeconomic implication?
**Correct:** To motivate effort (workers lose a valuable job if caught shirking), reduce turnover, and attract higher-quality applicants. Macroeconomic implication: efficiency wages create a wage floor → quantity of labor supplied exceeds quantity demanded → involuntary unemployment.
**Explanation:** This is one of the New Keynesian explanations for wage stickiness and persistent unemployment. Two workers with identical credentials may earn very different wages — one in an efficiency-wage job, one not.

## High-Yield Synthesis
- **Surplus capture & efficiency paradox:** Perfect price discrimination eliminates deadweight loss (efficient output!) while transferring 100% of consumer surplus to the monopolist. Memorize this counterintuitive result — monopoly + PD ≠ inefficiency.
- **Game-theory toolkit:** Master the circle trick (two circles in a row/column = dominant strategy; two circles in a cell = Nash equilibrium). Understand WHY one-shot prisoners' dilemma produces noncooperative Nash, but repeated interaction + tit-for-tat sustains tacit collusion. Antitrust law tilts toward defection by making formal agreements unenforceable.
- **Factor-market unified framework:** Every factor (land, labor, capital) is hired where MRP = MFC. Firm's MRP curve IS its factor demand curve. In competitive markets, MFC = factor price; in monopsony, MFC > price → wage below MRP → "exploitation." Cost-minimization rule (MPL/W = MPK/r) determines input MIX; MRP=MFC determines input QUANTITY. Wage inequality has 5 sources: compensating differentials, talent, human capital, market power (unions/monopsony), efficiency wages, and discrimination.
