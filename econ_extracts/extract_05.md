## Key Concepts (with mnemonics)

### Concept: Keynesian Economics
- **Definition:** Macro framework attributing business cycles to shifts in AD (often from "animal spirits"); justifies active stabilization policy.
- **Why it matters:** AP exams test the distinction from classical view: Keynes' SRAS slopes upward, so AD shifts move output.
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "Keynes Keeps you Keeping busy" — government keeps spending in recessions.
- **Related:** Macroeconomic policy activism, animal spirits, AD shock.

### Concept: Monetarism
- **Definition:** Doctrine (Milton Friedman) that GDP grows steadily if M grows steadily; central bank should follow a fixed-growth monetary rule.
- **Why it matters:** Common test trap: students confuse monetarism with classical economics — but monetarism still accepts short-run real effects of M.
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "Mr. Monetarist Marches the Money at a constant pace."
- **Related:** Quantity Theory of Money, monetary policy rule, velocity, Friedman, Schwartz.

### Concept: Velocity of Money
- **Definition:** Ratio of nominal GDP to money supply (V = PY/M); the average number of times a dollar turns over per year.
- **Why it matters:** If V is unstable, M-growth rule fails — this is why monetarism collapsed after 1980.
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "V = Vivacious dollar — how often it parties."
- **Related:** Quantity Theory of Money, M1, MV=PY.

### Concept: Natural Rate Hypothesis (Friedman–Phelps)
- **Definition:** To avoid accelerating inflation, the actual unemployment rate must equal the natural rate (NAIRU); any attempt to push U below NAIRU produces ever-rising inflation.
- **Why it matters:** Implies LR Phillips curve is vertical; trade-off only short-run; widely accepted.
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "NAIRU = No Accelerating Inflation Rate of Unemployment — Natural Always Returns Up."
- **Related:** Long-run Phillips curve, expected inflation, stagflation.

### Concept: Rational Expectations
- **Definition:** Households and firms optimally use *all* available information — including announced policy — when forming expectations.
- **Why it matters:** Implies anticipated monetary policy is neutral even in the short run; predicts immediate (not gradual) build-in of expected inflation.
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "Rational = Read the Fed press release."
- **Related:** New classical macroeconomics, Lucas critique, policy ineffectiveness.

### Concept: Real Business Cycle Theory
- **Definition:** Business cycles caused by fluctuations in TFP / technology growth — i.e., SRAS shocks, not AD shocks.
- **Why it matters:** Recovers the vertical-AS classical view; awarded 2004 Nobel (Kydland & Prescott).
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "RBC = Recessions from Bad Computers" (tech slowdowns).
- **Related:** Total factor productivity, new classical macro, supply shocks.

### Concept: New Keynesian Economics
- **Definition:** Argues market imperfections (menu costs, monopolistic price-setting) cause price stickiness, restoring Keynesian results despite rational expectations.
- **Why it matters:** Reconciles rational expectations with real-world non-neutrality of money.
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "Sticky prices, new Keynes."
- **Related:** Menu costs, monopolistic competition, price stickiness.

### Concept: Political Business Cycle
- **Definition:** Unnecessary volatility caused by politicians juicing the economy before elections.
- **Why it matters:** Justifies central-bank independence.
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "Nixon 1972 — the GOAT of political business cycles" (per Rogoff).
- **Related:** Discretionary policy, central-bank independence.

### Concept: Supply-Side Economics & Laffer Curve
- **Definition:** Belief that tax-rate cuts raise potential output enough that revenue may even rise; Laffer curve = revenue rises then falls as tax rates increase.
- **Why it matters:** Trick: at *low* tax rates, a cut REDUCES revenue; only above the peak does a cut raise revenue.
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "Laffer = Laughs at high tax rates" — bell-shaped curve.
- **Related:** Ronald Reagan, supply shocks, tax incentives.

### Concept: Ricardian Equivalence
- **Definition:** Households see government spending today as future taxes; they reduce private spending to save for the tax bill, neutralizing fiscal stimulus.
- **Why it matters:** Key anti-stimulus argument during the Great Recession.
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "Ricardo Recognizes the Receipt — saves for tomorrow's tax."
- **Related:** Fiscal stimulus, debt, crowding out.

### Concept: Rule of 70
- **Definition:** Years to double ≈ 70 / annual growth rate (in %).
- **Why it matters:** Quick estimation tool, but only works for *positive*, steady growth rates.
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "70 is heaven for doubling."
- **Related:** Real GDP per capita, long-run growth.

### Concept: Labor Productivity
- **Definition:** Output per worker (or per hour); real GDP / number employed.
- **Why it matters:** ~90% of LR per-capita GDP growth comes from productivity, not from putting more people to work.
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "Productivity = Per-worker Power."
- **Related:** Physical capital, human capital, technology.

### Concept: Physical Capital
- **Definition:** Manufactured goods (machines, buildings, infrastructure) used to produce other goods.
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "Physical = you can Kick it."
- **Related:** Investment spending, depreciation, aggregate production function.

### Concept: Human Capital
- **Definition:** Skill/education embodied in workers.
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "Human = the head — Schooling adds to it."
- **Related:** Education, growth accounting.

### Concept: Technology / TFP
- **Definition:** Technical means for producing goods; TFP = output achievable with given inputs.
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "TFP shifts the curve UP." (movement along = capital; shift = tech.)
- **Related:** Growth accounting, real business cycle theory, productivity curve.

### Concept: Diminishing Returns to Physical Capital
- **Definition:** Holding H and tech fixed, each extra unit of K/worker raises Y/worker by less than the previous unit.
- **Why it matters:** Trick: only "other things equal" — if H or tech rises, returns may NOT diminish.
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "Each tractor matters less than the first tractor."
- **Related:** Aggregate production function, growth accounting.

### Concept: Convergence Hypothesis
- **Definition:** Poorer countries should grow faster than richer ones, narrowing income gaps.
- **Why it matters:** Holds only conditionally — among wealthy/similar economies (Western Europe, Japan, U.S.) but NOT globally. "Conditional convergence" once you control for education, institutions, infrastructure.
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "Catch-up only if you cleaned up your conditions."
- **Related:** East Asian miracle, Latin America, Africa.

### Concept: Sustainable Growth
- **Definition:** Growth that can continue given limited natural resources and environmental impact.
- **Why it matters:** Resource scarcity self-corrects via prices; environmental externalities don't — they require government intervention.
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "Sustainable = Stays sustainable only with Smart policy."
- **Related:** Negative externality, carbon tax, cap and trade, Malthus.

### Concept: Balance of Payments — Current Account
- **Definition:** Trade in goods + services + factor income + net transfers. Transactions that don't create future liabilities.
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "Current = Cash on the spot for stuff/services/income."
- **Related:** Trade balance, financial account, BOP identity.

### Concept: Balance of Payments — Financial Account
- **Definition:** Sales/purchases of assets between residents and foreigners; creates future liabilities.
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "Financial = Future claims (assets)."
- **Related:** Current account, capital inflows.

### Concept: Appreciation / Depreciation
- **Definition:** Appreciation = currency gains value vs. another; depreciation = loses value.
- **Why it matters:** Common AP trap: when €/US$ rises, the dollar APPRECIATED and the euro depreciated.
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "More foreign per buck = buck appreciated."
- **Related:** Real exchange rate, forex market.

### Concept: Real Exchange Rate
- **Definition:** Nominal exchange rate × (Pdomestic / Pforeign) — adjusts for relative price-level differences.
- **Why it matters:** Trade flows respond to *real*, not nominal, exchange rates. A nominal depreciation matched by domestic inflation leaves competitiveness unchanged.
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "Real rate = real cheaper goods abroad."
- **Related:** Purchasing power parity, current account.

### Concept: Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)
- **Definition:** Nominal exchange rate that equates the cost of a common basket across countries.
- **Why it matters:** Predicts LR exchange rates, not short-run. Big Mac Index is a fun proxy.
- **Mnemonic / way to remember:** "Same Big Mac price = PPP."
- **Related:** Real exchange rate, Big Mac index.

## Important Formulas

### Formula: Velocity Equation / Quantity Theory of Money
- **Equation:** M × V = P × Y
- **Variables:** M = money supply; V = velocity; P = aggregate price level; Y = real GDP.
- **Units / sign conventions:** M in $; PY = nominal GDP in $. V dimensionless (turnovers/year).
- **Worked example:** If M = $4 trillion and V = 5, then nominal GDP = PY = $20 trillion. If real GDP Y = $18 trillion, then P (price index) ≈ 1.11.

### Formula: Rule of 70
- **Equation:** Doubling time (years) ≈ 70 / (annual growth rate in %)
- **Variables:** growth rate must be positive % per year.
- **Units / sign conventions:** Growth rate as a PERCENT (use 7, not 0.07). Only positive rates.
- **Worked example:** China grows real GDP/capita at 8.9% — doubles in 70/8.9 ≈ 7.9 years.

### Formula: Aggregate Production Function (Bosworth–Collins form)
- **Equation:** Y/L = T × (K/L)^0.4 × (H/L)^0.6
- **Variables:** Y/L = output per worker; T = TFP/technology; K/L = physical capital/worker; H/L = human capital/worker.
- **Units / sign conventions:** Exponents (0.4, 0.6) sum to 1 — constant returns to scale. Diminishing returns to each factor alone.
- **Worked example:** If K/L doubles (×2) holding H/L and T fixed, Y/L rises by 2^0.4 ≈ 1.32 — only +32%, not +100%.

### Formula: Growth Accounting (Capital Contribution)
- **Equation:** Productivity growth from K = (% growth in K/L) × (elasticity of Y/L w.r.t. K/L)
- **Variables:** Typical elasticity ≈ 0.33 (a 1% rise in K/L raises Y/L by 0.33%).
- **Units / sign conventions:** Percent per year.
- **Worked example:** K/L grows 3%/year, elasticity 0.33 → contribution to productivity = 3 × 0.33 = 1 percentage point/year.

### Formula: Balance of Payments Identity
- **Equation:** CA + FA = 0  (i.e., CA = −FA)
- **Variables:** CA = current account balance; FA = financial account balance.
- **Units / sign conventions:** Surplus positive, deficit negative. In real data, statistical discrepancy makes it ≈ 0 but not exact.
- **Worked example:** U.S. 2012: CA = −$440B (deficit), FA = +$447B (surplus); residual ≈ $7B statistical error.

### Formula: Real Exchange Rate
- **Equation:** Real ER = Nominal ER × (Pdomestic / Pforeign)
- **Variables:** Nominal ER expressed as foreign currency per unit of domestic; P = price indexes.
- **Units / sign conventions:** Rises = domestic goods more expensive abroad (real appreciation).
- **Worked example:** Nominal ER = 15 pesos/US$, P_US = 100, P_Mex = 150 → Real ER = 15 × (100/150) = 10. So even if nominal peso depreciated, real ER unchanged.

### Formula: Purchasing Power Parity
- **Equation:** PPP exchange rate = Pdomestic / Pforeign (price of basket / price of basket)
- **Variables:** Same basket priced in each country's currency.
- **Units / sign conventions:** Units = foreign currency per domestic.
- **Worked example:** Basket costs $100 in U.S., 1,000 pesos in Mexico → PPP = 10 pesos/US$.

## Practice Questions

### Q1 [EASY] [CONCEPTUAL] — Velocity equation
**Question:** Which equation correctly represents the Quantity Theory of Money used by monetarists?
**Choices:** A) M + V = P + Y  B) M × V = P × Y  C) M / V = P × Y  D) M × V = P / Y
**Correct:** B
**Explanation:** The Quantity Theory states MV = PY (nominal GDP). Choice A confuses addition with multiplication; C and D mishandle the operation, breaking the dollar-flow logic that V is *turnovers* of M producing total spending PY.

### Q2 [MEDIUM] [NUMERICAL] — Rule of 70
**Question:** If a country's real GDP per capita grows at 3.5% per year, approximately how many years until it doubles? *(Trick: don't use 70/0.035.)*
**Choices:** A) 2  B) 20  C) 35  D) 70  E) 200
**Correct:** B
**Explanation:** Use percentages directly: 70/3.5 = 20 years. Common error: 70/0.035 = 2,000 (wrong — you must input the growth rate as a percent, not a decimal).

### Q3 [MEDIUM] [CONCEPTUAL] — Monetarism vs. Classical
**Question:** Monetarists differ from classical economists in which key respect?
**Choices:** A) Monetarists believe short-run changes in AD affect aggregate output; classicals do not.  B) Monetarists believe fiscal policy is more effective than monetary policy.  C) Classicals believe in price stickiness; monetarists do not.  D) Monetarists endorse discretionary monetary policy.  E) Classicals endorse fiscal stimulus.
**Correct:** A
**Explanation:** Both reject discretionary fiscal stimulus, but monetarists (like Keynesians) accept short-run real effects of AD shifts. Classicals assume vertical SRAS, so AD shifts only the price level. B is backward; C reverses the schools; D is wrong — monetarists want a rule, not discretion.

### Q4 [HARD] [NUMERICAL] — Growth accounting
**Question:** In Erehwon, real GDP grew 5% per year. Physical capital grew 4% per year and the labor force was unchanged. Each 1% rise in K/L raises Y/L by 0.3%. What % of productivity growth came from rising K/L?
**Choices:** A) 12%  B) 24%  C) 30%  D) 60%  E) 80%
**Correct:** B
**Explanation:** With labor unchanged, K/L grew 4%. Capital's contribution = 4 × 0.3 = 1.2 percentage points. Productivity growth = real GDP growth (since L is fixed) = 5%. So 1.2/5 = 24%. Common trap: choosing 30% (the elasticity itself) or 60% (1.2/2).

### Q6 [HARD] [NUMERICAL] — Real exchange rate
**Question:** The nominal exchange rate is 20 pesos/US$. The U.S. price index is 100 and the Mexican price index is 200. What is the real peso/dollar exchange rate?
**Choices:** A) 5  B) 10  C) 20  D) 40  E) 200
**Correct:** B
**Explanation:** Real ER = Nominal × (P_US / P_Mex) = 20 × (100/200) = 10. Common error: multiply by P_Mex/P_US to get 40, but the formula uses the *domestic* price index (here U.S., since the rate is pesos per dollar) over the foreign one.

### Q7 [HARD] [CONCEPTUAL] — Convergence trick
**Question:** Which best describes the empirical status of the convergence hypothesis?
**Choices:** A) It holds across all countries — poorer countries always grow faster.  B) It holds only among already-wealthy economies; globally only conditional convergence (controlling for institutions) holds.  C) It is universally rejected by the data.  D) It implies all countries will reach U.S. real GDP per capita within 70 years.  E) It applies only to countries that have signed free-trade agreements.
**Correct:** B
**Explanation:** Among wealthy similar nations (Western Europe, Japan, U.S.), per-capita incomes have converged. Worldwide, Africa diverged from Western Europe. After adjusting for education, infrastructure, rule of law, etc., poorer countries DO grow faster — "conditional convergence." A is the common naive misreading; C is too strong.

### Q8 [HARD] [CONCEPTUAL] — Balance of payments identity
**Question:** A country runs a current account deficit of $500B. Which of the following must be true?
**Choices:** A) It has a financial account deficit of $500B.  B) It has a financial account surplus of $500B.  C) Its currency must be depreciating.  D) Its GDP must be shrinking.  E) Its merchandise trade balance must be zero.
**Correct:** B
**Explanation:** CA + FA = 0, so a $500B current account deficit must be matched by a $500B financial account surplus — net capital inflow. A is the sign-flip mistake; C and D are unrelated; E confuses trade balance with current account.

### Q9 [OLYMPIAD] [CONCEPTUAL] — Lucas critique trick
**Question:** Under rational expectations (Lucas), suppose the Fed pre-announces and credibly commits to expanding the money supply by 5% to lower unemployment. What happens?
**Choices:** A) Unemployment falls below the natural rate, then returns to it after a few years.  B) Unemployment falls permanently below the natural rate.  C) Expected inflation jumps immediately, the SRPC shifts up, and unemployment stays at the natural rate while inflation rises.  D) Real GDP rises permanently.  E) Velocity of money falls to offset the increase.
**Correct:** C
**Explanation:** Under rational expectations, anticipated monetary policy is fully built into expectations *immediately*, not "eventually." So the SRPC shifts up the moment the policy is announced — no temporary boost to output. The natural rate hypothesis (Friedman–Phelps) would say A (gradual). C is the rational-expectations sharpening of A. B and D contradict the natural rate. E is unrelated.

### Q10 [OLYMPIAD] [NUMERICAL] — Aggregate production function exponents
**Question:** Using Y/L = T × (K/L)^0.4 × (H/L)^0.6, suppose K/L grows 5%/year, H/L grows 2%/year, and TFP grows 1%/year. What is the approximate growth rate of Y/L?
**Choices:** A) 1%  B) 2.2%  C) 4.2%  D) 6.0%  E) 8.0%
**Correct:** C
**Explanation:** Log-linearize: g(Y/L) = g(T) + 0.4 g(K/L) + 0.6 g(H/L) = 1 + 0.4(5) + 0.6(2) = 1 + 2 + 1.2 = 4.2%. Common errors: summing simply (5+2+1 = 8% — choice E); ignoring TFP (3.2%); using equal weights (3.5%).

### Q11 [OLYMPIAD] [CONCEPTUAL] — Laffer curve trick
**Question:** The U.S. economy currently operates on the upward-sloping portion of the Laffer curve. A reduction in marginal tax rates will most likely:
**Choices:** A) Increase tax revenue because work incentives rise.  B) Leave tax revenue unchanged.  C) Decrease tax revenue, since on the upward-sloping portion, lower rates mean lower revenue.  D) Cause the Laffer curve to shift outward.  E) Eliminate the budget deficit immediately.
**Correct:** C
**Explanation:** On the upward portion (low rates), revenue rises with rates and falls with rate cuts. Only on the downward portion does a cut RAISE revenue. Supply-side claims about Reagan-era cuts assumed the U.S. was on the downward side; CBO estimates didn't support that. A is the classic supply-side error.

### Q12 [MEDIUM] [NUMERICAL] — Capital flow direction
**Question:** Country A has an equilibrium interest rate of 3%; Country B has 7%. With free capital flows and no risk differences, the international equilibrium interest rate will most likely:
**Choices:** A) Stay at 3% in A and 7% in B.  B) Equalize at some rate between 3% and 7%, e.g., 5%.  C) Fall to 0% in both.  D) Rise to 10% in both.  E) Diverge further.
**Correct:** B
**Explanation:** Capital flows from low-r country A to high-r country B until interest rates equalize at an intermediate rate. The figure 41.4 example used 2% UK and 6% US equalizing to 4%. A is the closed-economy answer.

## High-Yield Synthesis
- **Schools of macro thought converge on the modern consensus:** monetary policy is the main short-run stabilization tool; fiscal stimulus is reserved for liquidity-trap conditions; central banks should be independent; the natural rate hypothesis is universally accepted; the only remaining hot disputes are over unconventional monetary policy in the zero-lower-bound era.
- **Long-run growth is driven by productivity**, which depends on physical capital per worker, human capital per worker, and (most importantly) technology / TFP. The aggregate production function exhibits diminishing returns to each input alone but can shift upward indefinitely with technological progress. Convergence happens only conditionally — countries with similar institutions catch up; countries trapped by political instability, poor property rights, and bad infrastructure do not.
- **Open-economy macro is governed by the balance-of-payments identity (CA + FA = 0)** and the equilibrating role of the exchange rate. Real exchange rates (not nominal) drive trade flows; purchasing power parity is the LR anchor; capital flows respond to interest-rate differentials and equalize returns across countries.
